# Example: a two-page synthesis of a market-entry question

The question: should a SaaS company enter the Brazilian market next year. The
gathering produced findings of uneven quality. The synthesis leads with the
answer and is honest about which parts are solid.

## The conclusion, first and qualified

```
Conditionally yes, but not next year. The market opportunity is real and
growing (high confidence), local payment and tax requirements are a significant
but surmountable barrier (high confidence), and the competitive intensity is the
open question that should decide timing (low confidence, and the reason to wait
one quarter to establish it). Recommendation: a one-quarter validation before
committing, not a go/no-go now.
```

A reader who stops here has the answer and its main condition.

## Evidence, attributed and typed

```
market size and growth
  [fact] the addressable segment grew 22% year on year -> <industry body report,
  rung 2, 2025, table 3>, cross-checked against <independent analyst, rung 3>
  confidence: high (two independent sources, one primary)

payment and tax barrier
  [fact] local payment methods dominate; foreign card acceptance is low
  -> <central bank payment statistics, rung 1>
  [inference] this requires a local payment integration before launch, not after
  -> labelled inference from the fact above
  confidence: high on the fact, moderate on the timeline to integrate

competitive intensity
  [opinion] two sources call the market "crowded"; both are vendor blogs with an
  interest, and neither quantifies it -> flagged as an unverified claim
  confidence: low; this is the gap
```

## Confidence, per conclusion, not blanket

```
high      the opportunity exists and is growing
high      the payment/tax barrier is real and must be solved before launch
low       how crowded the market actually is, which is what decides timing
```

## Gap and its effect on the decision

```
gap          real competitive intensity could not be established from independent
             sources; the only claims trace to interested parties
effect        this is not a minor gap; it is the variable that separates "enter
             next year" from "wait". Hence the recommendation to validate it
             first rather than decide now.
```

## Finding and recommendation kept separate

```
finding        the market is attractive with a solvable barrier and an unknown
               competitive picture
recommendation  a one-quarter validation of competitive intensity before
               committing; a reader may accept the finding and choose to enter
               immediately anyway, and the report lets them see exactly what risk
               they would be taking by skipping the validation
```

## The lesson

The synthesis did not hide the weak part in confident prose. It named the low-
confidence piece as the decisive one, which turned a vague "maybe" into a
specific, cheap next step.
