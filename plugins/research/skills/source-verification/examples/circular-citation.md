# Example: a statistic that everyone cites and nobody sourced

The claim under verification: "companies that adopt <practice> see a 30 percent
increase in <metric>." It appears in dozens of articles, decks and vendor pages,
always as "studies show" or "research indicates", always the same number.

## Tracing to origin

```
source A  a vendor blog: "studies show a 30% increase"; cites source B
source B  an industry article: "research indicates 30%"; cites source C
source C  a consultancy report: "our analysis suggests up to 30%"; cites nothing
          external; the number is the consultancy's own, from an unpublished
          survey, with no method disclosed
```

Thirty sources, one origin, and the origin is an undisclosed survey by a party
that sells the practice.

## Testing the origin

```
authority     a consultancy that sells the practice; positioned to know, but
              interested
independence  none: the origin profits from the claim being believed
method        undisclosed; "up to 30%" is a ceiling, not an average, and the
              sample and conditions are not stated
recency       the report is five years old; nothing has independently replicated it
```

## The drift in repetition

```
origin said   "our analysis suggests up to 30%"
repetition said  "studies show a 30% increase"
drift          "up to 30%" (a ceiling) became "a 30% increase" (a typical result);
               "our analysis suggests" (one interested party) became "studies show"
               (a body of independent research); the hedge was dropped
```

## Verdict

```
circular and unverified. The claim traces to a single interested origin with an
undisclosed method, and gained both precision and false independence in
repetition. Reported as: "a widely repeated figure that traces to one interested,
unpublished source; it should not be relied on as an established effect."
```

## The value

The decision was whether to invest in the practice on the strength of the "30
percent" figure. The verification did not disprove the practice; it removed a
false foundation, so the decision rests on something real or on an honest
"unknown", not on a number that thirty sources borrowed from one.
