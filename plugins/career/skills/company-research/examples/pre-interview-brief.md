# Example: a pre-interview brief on a scale-up

The candidate has an interview at a 200-person B2B scale-up. The brief is built
from live sources, each fact dated and attributed, signals presented as signals.

## What they do and how they earn

```
product   a workflow-automation platform for mid-market operations teams
          (from their site and a product trial)
model     per-seat SaaS subscription, annual contracts (from their pricing page)
market    competes with two named larger incumbents (from their own comparison
          page, read as their claim, and one independent analyst note)
stage     Series B scale-up, ~200 people (from a dated funding announcement)
```

## Health signals, dated and sourced

```
funding    Series B of $40M raised 14 months ago (announcement, dated)
           signal: 14 months into a Series B with continued hiring is normal
           runway territory; not a concern on its own, worth one interview question
growth     job board shows 18 open roles across engineering and sales
           (live, dated today); consistent with expansion, not contraction
news       a customer win with a named enterprise logo, two months ago (press
           release); a positive growth signal
stability  no layoffs or leadership departures found in reporting; stated as
           "none found", not as "stable" (absence of a signal, not a verdict)
```

## Culture, read as a pattern

```
sources    42 employee reviews across two sites, plus the company's own careers page
pattern    a recurring, specific theme: "fast-paced, long hours around quarterly
           releases", mentioned in ~10 reviews; balanced by consistent praise for
           the engineering team's autonomy
not        one dramatic one-star review alleging a specific manager is treated as
           a question to raise carefully, not reported as the culture
useful     the release-crunch theme is specific enough to ask about honestly in
           the interview
```

## Role context

```
team      backend platform team, ~8 engineers, reports to a named eng manager
          (from the listing and a reachable org note)
why open  growth headcount (the listing says "as we scale"), not a backfill
stack     Go, Postgres, Kubernetes (from the listing); matches the candidate,
          feeds the technical prep
challenge scaling the automation engine as customer count grows (from their
          engineering blog); the real problem the role exists to solve
```

## Red flags, weighted

```
weigh     the release-crunch theme is a sourced pattern worth weighing against
          the candidate's own tolerance; framed as a concern, not a disqualifier
question  the one-star manager review is unverifiable; prepared as a careful
          question about team stability, not repeated as fact
overall   a healthy-looking scale-up with one real, askable culture question; the
          candidate has what they need to decide and to interview well
```

## The lesson

Every fact carries a source and a date. The runway question is a signal to ask
about, not a prediction of doom. The culture is a pattern across 42 reviews, not
one rant. The candidate walks in informed, with two honest questions the research
surfaced, and none of it is invented.
