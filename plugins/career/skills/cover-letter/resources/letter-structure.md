# Cover letter structure

Three or four short paragraphs, a page at most. Each has one job. None is filler.

## The paragraphs

```
1  The specific opening
   A real, true reason for this company and this role. Something about the
   company's work, product, or problem that the candidate genuinely connects to,
   and why. Never "I am writing to apply for". If the candidate has no genuine
   reason, a plain competent opening beats forced enthusiasm.

2  The evidence
   Two or three of the role's real needs (from the listing and company-research),
   each connected to the candidate's specific, supportable experience, with the
   concrete detail a CV bullet leaves out. Show the experience meeting the need;
   do not assert a trait.

3  The gap, only if one is visible
   Name it in one honest sentence, then turn to the bridge: the transferable
   strength, the fast track record, the real plan. Owned briefly, a gap reads as
   self-awareness. Hidden or over-apologised, it reads as a problem.

4  The close
   What the candidate offers and a simple next step. Confident and plain. Not
   "I would be grateful for the opportunity", which is servile and empty.
```

## Rules

- Salutation to a real person where company-research found one; a specific name
  beats "Dear Hiring Manager".
- The letter complements the CV; it does not repeat it. If a sentence just
  restates a CV bullet, cut it.
- Register matches the industry and company. Read the room from company-research.
- Every claim is supportable in the interview the letter is trying to earn.
- Length is the discipline: specificity is brief, and a busy reader rewards it.

## The swappability test
Could the company name be replaced with another company's and the letter still
make sense? If yes, it is a template and it is rewritten until the answer is no.
