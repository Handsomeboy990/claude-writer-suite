# Example: a template opening rewritten into a specific one

The candidate is applying to a climate-tech company for a backend role. Two
versions of the same letter's opening and body, showing what specificity does.

## The template version (refused)

```
Dear Hiring Manager,

I am writing to apply for the Backend Engineer position at your company. I am a
passionate and results-driven engineer with strong problem-solving skills and a
proven track record. I believe I would be a great fit for your team and would be
grateful for the opportunity to contribute.
```

Every sentence fits any company and any role. It says nothing. The company name
could be swapped and nothing would change.

## The specific version

```
Dear <name from company-research>,

Your engineering blog's post on cutting your carbon-accounting pipeline's compute
cost by two thirds is the kind of problem I left my last role wanting more of: I
spent the past year rebuilding a billing pipeline for exactly that mix of
correctness-critical and cost-sensitive processing, and I want to do it for
something that measures its output in tonnes of CO2 rather than dollars.

Your posting asks for someone who can own reliability at scale and mentor a
growing team. When I led our monolith-to-services migration, I cut deploy time
from forty minutes to six and reduced incidents by a third, and I did it while
mentoring four engineers, two of whom were promoted. That combination, shipping
reliability while growing the people around me, is what I would bring.

I have not worked in climate tech before, so the domain would be new to me. What
is not new is picking up a domain fast: I went from knowing nothing about payment
systems to owning our billing infrastructure in under a year, and I would do the
same here.

I would welcome the chance to talk about your pipeline work.
```

## What makes it work

```
opening      a real, specific reason (the blog post, the CO2 framing) that is
             true; the candidate genuinely connects to it
evidence     two real, quantified achievements tied to the role's two stated needs
             (reliability at scale, mentoring), with detail the CV compresses
gap          the domain gap (no climate-tech experience) owned in one sentence,
             bridged with a real precedent (the payments ramp)
register     plain, confident, matched to an engineering-led company
length       four short paragraphs, under a page
close        simple, not servile
```

## The test

Find-and-replace the company name and the blog reference: the letter breaks,
because the specifics were real. That breakage is the proof the letter was
written for this role and no other.
