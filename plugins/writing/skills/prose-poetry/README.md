# prose-poetry

The prose poem: compact block, four cohesion forces (rhythm, lexical field,
sensory progression, closure), families of form, a seven-step procedure, and
the traps of lyricism and the explanatory ending.

- Inputs: a single place, instant or object.
- Outputs: prose poems.
- Depends on: `writing-constitution`, `poet`.

## When to use

For poetic prose, a notation, or a fragment.

## What distinguishes it

It does not progress; it deepens. A prose poem that acquires a plot has become
a short story, which section 5 lists as a trap rather than a variation.

## The part that takes longest

The last sentence. Section 6 asks for at least five rewrites of it, because
closure by displacement rather than conclusion is what separates the form from
a paragraph.
