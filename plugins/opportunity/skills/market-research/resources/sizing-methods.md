# Market sizing methods

Size a market so the number can guide a real decision and survive a hard question.
Prefer bottom-up; use top-down only as context; never use "we only need X percent"
as a method.

## Bottom-up (preferred)
Build the size from real units:
```
number of potential customers  (from a real source: a directory, a registry, an
                                association's membership, a census)
  x  realistic price            (benchmarked against real comparable products)
  x  realistic adoption          (an honest fraction who would actually buy, with
                                  the method for the estimate stated)
  =  serviceable revenue
```
Every input is sourced or is an estimate with its assumptions labelled. The result
is defensible because each step can be checked.

## The three market figures (never conflate them)
```
total market (TAM)        everyone who could conceivably use something in this
                          category; context only, usually irrelevant to the decision
serviceable market (SAM)  the part the provider can actually serve, given their
                          offering, geography, and reach
winnable share (SOM)      what they could realistically win in a few years against
                          real competition; the figure that guides the decision
```
Presenting the TAM as the opportunity is the classic overstatement. Decisions are
made against the winnable share.

## Top-down (context only)
A published "the market is X billion" figure sets context but does not size the
opportunity. It says nothing about what is reachable or winnable. Use it to orient,
never as the answer, and always with its source.

## The warning signs
```
"we only need 1% of a huge market"   not a method; a wish. What is the plan to get
                                     the 1%, and is 1% even reachable?
a market figure with no source        replace it with a bottom-up estimate whose
                                     assumptions are stated
"no competitors"                      find the substitutes; customers solve the
                                     problem somehow today
a single confident number             real sizing is a range with a confidence
                                     level and a method
```

## State confidence and method
Every figure carries either a source or an explicit estimation method and its
assumptions, and a confidence level. A number a real budget will be committed
against must be traceable to something checkable, or it is a fantasy dressed as
analysis.
