# Example: bottom-up sizing versus the top-down fantasy

A founder wants to build a scheduling tool for independent music teachers. Two
ways to size the market; only one survives contact with a real decision.

## The top-down fantasy (refused)

```
"The global education-software market is $300 billion. The music-education segment
is $5 billion. If we capture just 1% of that, we have a $50 million business."
```

Every step is a leap. The $300B includes universities and textbooks. The "$5B
music education" is unsourced and includes instruments and venues. The "1%" is a
wish, not a plan. This number cannot survive an investor question or guide a real
decision.

## The bottom-up sizing (defensible)

```
who exactly    independent music teachers who take individual students and manage
               their own schedules; not schools, not academies with admin staff
how many       from a teacher-directory source and a professional association's
               membership figures (both live, dated): an estimated 200,000-300,000
               in the target countries. [moderate confidence: two sources, a range]
who would pay   of those, the subset frustrated enough with current tools (paper,
               generic calendars) to pay: hard to know precisely. From reviews of
               adjacent tools and forum complaints, a meaningful minority express
               the pain. Estimate 15-25% as reachable-and-willing. [low confidence,
               method stated: inferred from complaint signals, not measured]
price           a realistic monthly price for a solo professional tool, benchmarked
               against comparable tools: $8-12/month. [moderate, from real pricing]
serviceable     200,000 x 20% x $10/month x 12 = ~$48M/year total serviceable
               revenue if fully captured, which no one does
winnable        realistically winning 2-5% of the serviceable market in a few years
               against existing generic tools: $1-2.4M/year. [low-moderate, the
               honest winnable figure]
```

## What changed

```
the fantasy said $50M by capturing "1% of a huge market"
the bottom-up said ~$1-2.4M winnable, built from real teacher counts, real prices,
and an honestly-labelled willingness estimate
```

The bottom-up figure is smaller and real. It can guide a decision (is a $1-2M
niche worth this founder's time?) where the fantasy cannot.

## The competitive reality

```
"no direct competitor" -> false. The substitutes are the real competition:
generic calendars, paper, and two adjacent scheduling tools not built for music
teachers. Customers already solve this somehow; the gap is the music-specific
workflow those substitutes handle poorly, and that gap is the real opportunity.
```

## The honest assessment

```
a real but small niche (~$1-2.4M winnable), with a genuine gap (music-specific
workflow) against weak substitutes, reachable through teacher directories and
associations. Moderate confidence on the size, low on the willingness. For a solo
founder wanting a sustainable small business: viable. For one seeking a venture-
scale outcome: too small, and the research says so plainly.
```

## The lesson

The top-down fantasy would have justified anything and guided nothing. The
bottom-up sizing produced a smaller, honest number that actually answers the
founder's decision, and the "too small for venture scale" finding is exactly the
kind of expensive mistake honest market research exists to prevent.
