# Example: a two-minute hackathon pitch, structured

The accessibility browser extension from hackathon-strategy, pitched in two
minutes to judges scoring impact 40%, working demo 30%, execution 20%,
presentation 10%. The structure serves that rubric.

## The pitch, timed

```
0:00-0:25  PROBLEM and STAKES (serves impact, 40%)
  "Ninety-eight percent of the web has an accessibility failure a screen-reader
  user hits: an image with no description, text they cannot read, a form they
  cannot navigate by keyboard. For millions of people, most of the internet is
  partly broken, every day. I'm going to show you a fix that works on any page,
  right now."

  Opens with the problem, made concrete and felt, with a stake (millions, daily).
  No technology yet. The judge cares before they see the solution.

0:25-1:25  DEMO (serves working demo, 30%, the heaviest realistic points)
  "Here's a real news site with three of those failures." [live]
  "I click the extension." [live] "It just added descriptions to every image,
  fixed the contrast on this unreadable caption, and made this menu keyboard-
  navigable. Same page, thirty seconds, no developer needed."

  Shows the working thing doing the real thing, following the problem. A visible
  before/after. The single most persuasive minute in the room.

1:25-1:45  HONEST SCOPE (serves credibility)
  "Today it handles alt text, contrast, and keyboard nav. It uses an on-demand
  vision model for the descriptions. Next is form-labelling and a personalisation
  layer, which we designed but did not build this weekend."

  What works today, honestly; what is next, framed as next, not implied as done.

1:45-2:00  THE ONE THING and CLOSE
  "The web will never be fully accessible one site at a time. This fixes any page
  for the person who needs it, on their side. That's the idea we want you to
  remember." [stop, on time]
```

## What makes it score

```
impact       the problem-and-stakes opening and the "any page" framing hit the
             heaviest criterion directly
working demo the live before/after is the whole middle, showing not telling
execution    the demo running cleanly evidences it
presentation problem-first structure, one memorable idea, on time
honesty      the "next" features are named as next; nothing faked is claimed
```

## The delivery plan

```
rehearsed    run against a two-minute timer three times; the first was 2:40 and
             cut the roadmap slide that did not serve the arc
fallback     a recorded screen capture of the demo, ready, in case the venue wifi
             fails mid-pitch
questions    prepared honest answers to "does the vision model cost money per
             image" (yes, and here is the tradeoff) and "what did you build vs use"
```

## The lesson

Two minutes, and over half of it is the working demo, because that is where the
points and the persuasion are. The pitch opens on the problem, not the vision
model, and it names the unbuilt features as future rather than faking them. That
honesty and that structure place it above flashier pitches that opened on
architecture and demoed slides.
