# Structure of a research synthesis

The order that serves a reader who must act, and check. Conclusion first,
evidence in support, honesty about confidence and gaps throughout.

## The sections

```
1  Answer            the conclusion to the question, in the first lines, with its
                     confidence and conditions. A reader who stops here is served.
2  Confidence map    which parts of the answer are known firmly and which are not;
                     per conclusion, never one blanket hedge
3  Evidence          the supporting findings, each attributed and typed
                     (fact / inference / opinion), weighted by how much it carries
4  Disagreements     where sources conflicted, presented, not resolved silently
5  Gaps              what could not be established, and what it means for the
                     decision: does it change the answer, or only its confidence
6  Recommendation    if asked, bounded to the evidence, with the alternatives a
                     different weighting or a filled gap would produce
7  Evidence trail    the sources and their locations, so any claim can be checked
```

## Rules

- Section 1 exists even in a long report. The conclusion is never withheld to the
  end.
- A finding that carries the conclusion is presented prominently; a minor finding
  is not given equal space.
- Confidence words are used precisely: high, moderate, low, each meaning what
  research-core defines, not as decoration.
- The recommendation (section 6) is separable from the finding (sections 1 to 5).
  A reader may accept the finding and reject the recommendation; the structure
  lets them.
- Length matches the decision. A high-stakes decision earns a full structure; a
  small question earns sections 1, 3, and 5 and nothing more.

## The test
Read only section 1. Does it answer the question, and does it signal its own
uncertainty honestly? If yes, the synthesis has done its job for the reader in a
hurry, and the rest serves the reader who checks.
