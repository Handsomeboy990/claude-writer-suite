# Verification tests

Apply to any claim a decision rests on. Each test is a question; a bad answer is
a reason to lower the verdict, not to discard the test.

## Provenance
- [ ] Can the claim be traced to a specific origin, not just "sources say"?
- [ ] Does the origin state the claim as its own finding, or cite it onward?
- [ ] Has the claim drifted from the origin: gained precision, lost a hedge,
      turned association into causation, turned "up to" into "typically"?

## The origin's authority
- [ ] Is the origin in a position to actually know the claim?
- [ ] Is the origin independent of an interest in the claim being true?
- [ ] For an empirical claim, does the disclosed method support the conclusion,
      or is the conclusion larger than the method allows?
- [ ] Is the origin current, or superseded by later work?
- [ ] Has the origin been corrected, retracted, or contradicted by its author?

## Independence and cross-check
- [ ] Is there a source that supports the claim without tracing to the same origin?
- [ ] Do independent sources reach the claim by different routes (converging), or
      by repeating the same wording (circular)?
- [ ] Should this claim be widely documented? If so, does its scarcity worry you?

## The verdict
- [ ] verified: sound origin, supported, independently cross-checked
- [ ] partly: origin supports a narrower version; report the narrower one
- [ ] unverified: no sound origin found; report as unverified, do not upgrade
- [ ] contradicted: a sound source disagrees; report the contradiction
- [ ] circular: many sources, one unverified origin; report that as the finding

## The discipline
The test you are most tempted to skip is the one on the claim that best fits the
conclusion you want. Run it there first.
