# Example: turning a vague ask into a researchable question

A request arrives as "find out about the EU AI regulation and whether it affects
us". It is three questions wearing one coat. Research-core separates them before
any source is opened.

## The vague ask, decomposed

```
1  What does the EU AI Act actually require, and of whom?
2  Does our product fall into a regulated category, and which risk tier?
3  What would compliance require of us, and by when?
```

Each has a different answer shape and a different source at the top of the ladder.

## Question 1: the requirement

```
scope        the Act as enacted, not proposals or commentary
answer shape a description of obligations by risk tier
top source   primary: the text of the Regulation itself, official journal
not          a law firm's blog summarising it, which is orientation only
decision     informs whether questions 2 and 3 are worth pursuing
```

## Question 2: does it apply to us

```
scope        our product's actual function, matched to the Act's categories
answer shape a tier assignment with the reasoning
top source   primary: the Act's category definitions, applied to our product
inference    this step is inference from fact, and is labelled as such: the Act
             says X, our product does Y, therefore the tier is likely Z
caveat       a legal determination is a lawyer's call; research produces the
             analysis, flagged as not legal advice
```

## Question 3: what compliance requires

```
scope        the obligations for the tier established in question 2
answer shape a list of required actions with deadlines
top source   primary: the Act's timeline and obligations for that tier
gap          if the implementing standards are not yet published, say so, and
             say that the detail depends on them
```

## What the framing produced

Three researchable questions, each with a stated scope, answer shape, and top
source, and one explicit boundary (legal determination is not research's to
make). The vague ask would have produced a plausible-sounding summary that mixed
the enacted text with a blog's paraphrase and the researcher's guess about the
tier. The framing keeps those three separate and labelled.
