# Pitch structure

The spine that wins a hackathon, a demo day, and an investor pitch alike. The
proportions shift with the audience; the order does not.

## The arc

```
1  Problem        open here. A concrete problem a real person has, made vivid
                  enough that the audience feels it before they see the solution.
                  Never open on the technology or the team.

2  Stakes         why it matters: who is affected, how much, why now. This is the
                  impact story most rubrics and every investor weight heavily.

3  Solution       the answer to the problem just felt, in one clear sentence
                  before any detail. The build serves the story.

4  Demo           show it working. The actual product doing the actual thing,
                  following the problem-to-solution arc. "Let me show you", not
                  "imagine if". Demo the part that works and scores; never the
                  fragile feature.

5  Honest scope   what works today, what is next. Framed truthfully: future work
                  is "next", not implied as done. Honesty here buys credibility
                  for everything else.

6  The one thing  the single idea the audience should leave remembering. Named,
                  and the pitch built so it lands.
```

## Rules

- Fit the time limit exactly. A pitch cut off before the demo is a loss. Rehearse
  against the clock; the first run is always too long.
- Show, do not tell. The working demo is the most persuasive element; give it the
  most time.
- Cut everything off the arc: the architecture deep-dive, the full roadmap, the
  team history, the acknowledgements.
- Every claim is true. A caught overclaim discounts the whole pitch. A demo shows
  real behaviour, not a hardcoded path presented as general.
- Prepare a demo fallback (a recording) and honest answers to the obvious
  questions.

## Adapting to the audience
```
hackathon judge   weight the demo and the impact; fit the tight time
demo day          weight the traction and the vision; the demo still leads
investor          weight the market, the model, and the team; the problem-first
                  spine and the working demo still open, then the business case
internal review   weight the fit to the goal and the next steps
```
The emphasis moves; the problem-first, show-do-not-tell, honest-claims spine is
constant.
