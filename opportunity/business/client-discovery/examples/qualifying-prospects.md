# Example: from an offering to a qualified shortlist

A freelance developer offers "backend rescue: fixing and stabilising slow or
buggy APIs for small SaaS teams." The discovery defines who fits and finds real,
signalled prospects.

## Ideal client profile, from the real offering

```
fits        small SaaS companies (10-50 people), post-launch, with a live product
            and paying customers, whose team is stretched
signal      an observable sign of API pain now: public complaints about slowness,
            a job post for a backend engineer they cannot fill, a recent funding
            round driving growth their backend was not built for, an outage
            postmortem mentioning the API
excludes    pre-launch startups (no live API to rescue), large companies with
            their own platform teams, non-technical products
```

## Real, signalled prospects (from live sources)

```
1  <Company A>   fits: 30-person SaaS, live product
   signal: three recent public reviews mention "the app is getting slow"; a
   six-week-old unfilled "Senior Backend Engineer" job post
   -> strong fit, strong current signal
reachability: the CTO is active and reachable on a professional network -> high

2  <Company B>   fits: 15-person SaaS
   signal: raised a Series A two months ago (announcement); growth-driven load is
   the developer's exact scenario
   -> strong fit, good signal (growth implies future pain, not proven current pain)
reachability: a founder with a public contact -> high

3  <Company C>   fits the profile on size and sector
   signal: none found; no complaints, no relevant hiring, no growth event
   -> fits but NO signal; marked lower priority, a name not yet a prospect

4  <Company D>   a large enterprise
   -> excluded by the profile: has its own platform team; not a fit
```

## Qualified shortlist, prioritised

```
priority 1  Company A: strong fit, current signal (slowness complaints + unfilled
            backend role), reachable CTO. The developer's offering answers exactly
            the signal. Approach angle: reference the public slowness signal and
            the hiring gap, offer the rescue as the faster fix than a hire.
priority 2  Company B: strong fit, growth signal, reachable founder. Approach angle:
            "post-Series-A growth often exposes API limits; here is how I help."
watch       Company C: fits but no signal; not worth outreach now. Revisit if a
            signal appears.
drop        Company D: excluded by profile.
```

## Effort guidance

```
Spend the outreach on Company A first (current, proven signal) and Company B
second (strong growth signal). Two well-qualified, signalled prospects with real
contacts and a specific angle beat a hundred SaaS company names. Company C is a
name, not a prospect, until it shows a signal.
```

## The lesson

The profile followed the real offering, so it excluded the enterprise and the
pre-launch startup that would not convert. The signal separated the two real
prospects (A and B) from the mere fit (C). Every organisation, contact, and signal
is from a live source; none is invented, because the developer will spend real
hours on outreach to exactly these.
