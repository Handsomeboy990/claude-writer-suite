# Example: a template message rewritten from real research

The provider (the backend-rescue freelancer) approaches Company A from
client-discovery. Two versions of the outreach show what lead research does.

## The template (refused)

```
Hi,

I hope this email finds you well. I am a backend developer who helps companies
like yours improve their systems. I have a lot of experience and would love to
help you with your backend needs. Do you have time for a call this week?
```

Nothing here is specific to Company A. It could go to any company. A busy CTO
deletes it.

## The research, from live sources

```
organisation  Company A, 30-person SaaS; three recent public reviews mention the
              app getting slow; a six-week-old unfilled "Senior Backend Engineer"
              posting (all from live sources, dated)
person        the CTO, named, role current, active on a professional network;
              recently posted publicly about "scaling pains as we grow" (public,
              professional, real)
reason now    the slowness complaints + the unfilled role + the CTO's own public
              post about scaling pains: a strong, current, true reason
channel       the professional network where the CTO is active and posts; a real,
              reachable channel, not a guessed email
```

## The grounded outreach (drafted, not sent)

```
Hi <name>,

I saw your post about scaling pains as the team grows, and noticed you have had a
senior backend role open for a while. I do exactly one thing: I go into a small
SaaS backend that has started to feel slow, find why, and stabilise it, usually in
a few weeks, so you get the relief a new hire would bring months sooner while you
keep looking for the right person.

A couple of your recent app reviews mention slowness, so I suspect there is a
concrete bottleneck I could pin down quickly. If it is useful, I can take a look
and tell you what I would do, no obligation.

Worth a short call?
```

Every specific in it is real: the CTO's public post, the open role, the slowness
reviews. The offer is honest about what the provider does and does not oversell.

## Prepared, not sent

```
The draft is handed to the provider. It is NOT sent on their behalf. The provider
reviews it, adjusts the voice to their own, and sends it themselves, or confirms
that specific message for sending. Nothing goes out automatically.
```

## The lesson

The template said "companies like yours"; the researched version named the CTO's
own words, the open role, and the review signal. The difference is entirely in the
sourced research: real facts about a real person, used to open a real conversation
about a real need. And it is drafted for the provider to send, never sent silently
on their behalf.
