# Opportunity evaluation criteria

The axes to score any opportunity on: an idea, a hackathon, a lead, a market
segment. Named before scoring, weighted by the binding constraint, each score
carrying its reasoning.

## The axes

```
feasibility     can this actor actually do it, with their skills, resources and time
fit             does it match this actor's goals and strengths, not an abstract ideal
value           does it deliver real value to a real intended audience
cost            what it takes: money, time, effort, opportunity cost
risk            what could go wrong, and how bad; the downside, not just the upside
constraint      the one that binds hardest: time, budget, eligibility, skill,
                distribution, authorisation; an opportunity that fails it is out
```

## Scoring discipline

- Name the axes and the binding constraint before looking at the options, so the
  options do not select flattering criteria.
- Score each option on each axis with a one-line reason, not a bare number. "3:
  feasible but needs a design skill the team lacks" is useful; "3" is not.
- Weight by the binding constraint. An option that fails it scores out regardless
  of how well it does elsewhere. A brilliant idea the team cannot distribute, a
  perfect hackathon the person is not eligible for, a lucrative lead outside the
  service offering: all out.
- Evaluate for the real actor. The same option scores differently for a solo
  part-timer and a funded team.

## From scores to a shortlist

- Rank the survivors of the binding constraint by the weighted axes.
- Recommend a few, each with why it made the cut and what it would demand.
- Show the notable rejects and why, so the actor sees the space was explored.
- Give each shortlisted option a concrete next step: the test, the application,
  the outreach.
- If nothing scores well after honest evaluation, say so. A weak field reported
  honestly is a real finding; a manufactured strong recommendation is a trap.

## The reasoning test
For every score, state the sentence behind it. If the sentence cannot be stated,
the score is a guess and the evaluation is decoration. The reasoning is the
output; the number is shorthand for it.
