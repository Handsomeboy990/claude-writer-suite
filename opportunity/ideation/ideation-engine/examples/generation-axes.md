# Example: generating with axes instead of a flat brainstorm

The problem, framed first: "small independent landlords struggle to track which
tenant paid which month, and chase late rent, using spreadsheets and memory." The
audience: independent landlords with two to ten units, not property-management
firms. A flat brainstorm would produce ten "rent tracker app" variations. Axes
force real range.

## Axis 1: mechanism (how the tracking happens)

```
- a spreadsheet template with automation (minimal, meets them where they are)
- a mobile app with reminders (the obvious middle)
- a bank-connected tool that detects the payment automatically (ambitious, higher
  trust and integration cost)
- a shared ledger the tenant also sees and confirms (shifts work to the tenant)
```

## Axis 2: audience-within-the-audience

```
- the landlord who wants the least software (the spreadsheet variant wins here)
- the landlord growing past what memory handles (the app variant)
- the landlord who wants tenants to self-serve (the shared-ledger variant)
```

## Axis 3: business model

```
- one-time paid template
- monthly SaaS per unit
- free tool, paid only for the bank-connection tier
```

## Axis 4: inversion (the opposite of the default)

```
default    software the landlord operates
inverted   a tool the TENANT operates that keeps the landlord informed, flipping
           who does the data entry; surfaces the assumption that the landlord
           must be the user, which the default hid
```

## The diverse set handed over (near-duplicates collapsed)

```
1  automated spreadsheet template, one-time paid  [minimal, low trust barrier]
   premise (hypothesis): landlords want tracking without adopting new software
2  mobile app with reminders, SaaS per unit       [the middle]
   premise (observed in forums): late-rent chasing is the real pain, not tracking
3  bank-connected auto-detection, tiered           [ambitious]
   premise (hypothesis): the payment-matching step is what they hate most
4  tenant-operated ledger, landlord informed       [inverted]
   premise (hypothesis): tenants will self-report to avoid being chased
```

Four ideas that differ in mechanism, audience, model, and even who the user is,
each carrying its premise marked observed or hypothesised. Not ten names for idea 2.

## The lesson

The axes did the work a flat brainstorm cannot: they forced the minimal option
(1), the ambitious one (3), and the inversion (4) into view, none of which a
"list rent-tracker ideas" prompt produces. Evaluation now chooses between real
alternatives, and the one observed premise (late-rent chasing is the pain) is
flagged as the strongest starting point.
