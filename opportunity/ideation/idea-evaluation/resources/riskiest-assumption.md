# Finding and testing the riskiest assumption

Every idea has one assumption that, if false, sinks the whole thing. Evaluation's
sharpest tool is to find it and test it cheaply, before committing real effort.

## Finding it

List everything that has to be true for the idea to succeed. Then ask, for each:
- How sure am I that this is true?
- If it turned out false, does the idea survive?

The riskiest assumption is the one that scores low on certainty and high on
consequence: the thing you are least sure of that would sink the idea if wrong.
It is usually one of:

```
demand        the audience actually has this need and feels it strongly
willingness   they will pay (or switch, or change behaviour) to solve it
reach         you can actually get the product in front of them
feasibility   the hard technical or operational part can actually be done
```

Most ideas fail on demand or willingness, not on feasibility, yet most effort
goes to feasibility because it is the fun part. Test the demand assumption first.

## Testing it cheaply

The test is measured in days, not months, and it targets the riskiest assumption
specifically:

```
demand / willingness
  a landing page with a clear offer and a signal button, shared where the audience is
  ten real conversations with the target audience
  a search of existing solutions and their reviews (what people already pay for
  and complain about is strong evidence)
  a pre-order, a waitlist, a "notify me" that measures intent, not just interest

feasibility
  a time-boxed spike on the one hard part, not the whole build
  a proof of concept of the risky integration only
```

## The ordering rule

Test the riskiest assumption first. De-risking the easy parts of an idea whose
hard part is unproven is wasted motion. If the riskiest assumption fails the
cheap test, the idea is redirected or dropped, and the effort that would have
built it is saved. If it holds, the idea has earned the next, larger investment.

## What this produces
Not a decision to build, but a decision about what to learn next, and the cheap
experiment that will teach it. That is the honest output of evaluating an idea
that still contains an untested assumption, which every unbuilt idea does.
