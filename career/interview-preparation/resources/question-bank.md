# Interview question bank

The questions to drill, by kind. For each, the candidate has a real, structured
answer before the interview, and has rehearsed the follow-ups.

## Behavioural (map each to a real story)
- Tell me about a time you solved a hard technical problem.
- Tell me about a conflict with a colleague and how you handled it.
- Tell me about a failure and what you learned.
- Tell me about a time you led without authority.
- Tell me about a deadline you had to meet under pressure.
- Tell me about a decision you made with incomplete information.

Each answer uses the structure: situation, what you did, the result, what you
learned. Each is a real experience. One strong story may answer several of these
from different angles; map which covers which.

## Technical (at the role's real level)
- The core competencies the role lists, drilled honestly including the hard ones.
- A walk-through of a real system the candidate built, with the trade-offs.
- The domain-specific ground from company-research (their stack, their problems).

## The "why" questions (consistent with the cover letter)
- Why this company? (the real, specific reason)
- Why this role? (the honest fit and direction)
- Why are you leaving / why did you leave? (honest, never bitter)

## The hard questions (rehearsed honestly)
- You do not have experience with X. (the honest bridge, in the candidate's voice)
- What is your greatest weakness? (a real, non-fatal one with a genuine mitigation)
- What are your salary expectations? (aligned to the configured floor, never invented)
- Tell me about a real failure. (a true one, learned from, no humblebrag)

## The candidate's own questions (prepared, real)
- What does success in this role look like in the first six months?
- What is the team's biggest current technical challenge?
- How are decisions made / how is the team structured?
- Questions specific to what company-research surfaced.

## The rule for the whole bank
Every answer is grounded in something the candidate actually did or genuinely
thinks. The mock presses each with follow-ups. An answer that cannot survive
"why did you do it that way" is re-drilled until it can, or it is a sign the
underlying claim was overstated and needs correcting at the source.
