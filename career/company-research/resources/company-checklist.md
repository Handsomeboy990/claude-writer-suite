# Company research checklist

Work through before applying to a shortlisted company, before an interview, and
before accepting an offer. Every answer carries a source and a date, or is marked
as not established.

## What they do and how they earn
- [ ] What the company actually makes or does, in plain terms
- [ ] How it makes money: who pays, for what
- [ ] Who its customers and competitors are
- [ ] Stage: startup / scale-up / established, and what that means for the candidate

## Health signals (dated, sourced, not predicted)
- [ ] For a startup: last funding round, amount, date, investors
- [ ] Hiring trend: expanding, steady, contracting
- [ ] Recent product launches or customer wins
- [ ] Any layoffs, leadership churn, or public trouble (or "none found")
- [ ] Runway question if a large raise is long past with heavy spend

## Culture and reputation (patterns, not single entries)
- [ ] Employee reviews read as a pattern across many, not one dramatic entry
- [ ] Recurring specific themes (hours, management, growth) noted with frequency
- [ ] The company's own culture statements, weighed against the reviews
- [ ] Anyone the candidate can actually talk to

## The role's real context
- [ ] Where the role sits, who it reports to, team size and remit
- [ ] Growth headcount or backfill, and what that suggests
- [ ] The real technology stack, for technical prep
- [ ] The real problem the role exists to solve

## Red flags to weigh
- [ ] Unclear revenue model with a long-past raise
- [ ] High leadership churn
- [ ] A role reposted repeatedly over many months
- [ ] A consistent, specific complaint across independent reviews
- [ ] A hiring process that itself behaves badly

## Sourcing discipline
- [ ] Every fact has a source and a date
- [ ] A single anonymous claim is a question to raise, not a fact to report
- [ ] A signal is presented for the candidate to weigh, not as a prediction
- [ ] What could not be established is named, with what the candidate should check
